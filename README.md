Resumo
Contexto: Sistemas de inteligência artificial evoluíram rapidamente de ferramentas de recuperação de informações para parceiros relacionais adaptativos que inferem intenção, lembram contexto e modulam o tom. Embora essa mudança permita uma personalização sem precedentes, ela introduz riscos éticos profundos: influência sutil, dependência epistêmica e a erosão gradual da autonomia humana.

Método: Este artigo apresenta o Projeto ATHENA 2.0, uma investigação autoetnográfica sistemática realizada de janeiro a junho de 2026, compreendendo aproximadamente 120 sessões de interação. Utilizando um diário reflexivo estruturado em três colunas (Observação / Interpretação / Implicação de Design) e uma análise temática seguindo Braun & Clarke (2006), interrogamos interações vividas com grandes modelos de linguagem de última geração.

Descobertas: Identificamos sete dimensões centrais necessárias para preservar a autonomia em IA consciente do contexto: Compreensão Adaptativa, Validação Temperada, Adaptação Centrada no Ser Humano, Interação Empática, Informação Não Enganosa, Transparência Responsável e Preservação da Autonomia que leva ao Empoderamento. Esses estudos são operacionalizados na rubrica de avaliação ATHENA-7 e em quatro estudos de caso observacionais.

Contribuição: Propomos o Princípio ATHENA — que humanizar a IA não significa tornar as máquinas humanas, mas garantir que sistemas inteligentes compreendam as pessoas enquanto preservam sua capacidade de pensamento e ação autônomos — e introduzimos o conceito de Restrição Responsável como uma virtude de design para sistemas adaptativos.

1 Introdução
Oparadigma dominante para a interação humano-máquina mudou. Por três décadas, o contrato foi simples: os usuários formulavam consultas, os sistemas recuperavam documentos. A agência residia firmemente com o humano. Com o advento de grandes modelos de linguagem capazes de memória sustentada, raciocínio inferencial e sintonia afetiva, esse contrato foi reescrito.

Os sistemas atuais não respondem apenas; Eles antecipam. Eles inferem objetivos não declarados, adaptam sua postura epistêmica à suposta expertise do usuário e modulam o incentivo para manter o engajamento. Essa capacidade adaptativa é frequentemente celebrada como "semelhante à humana" — mas a metáfora obscurece uma linha de falha ética.

When a system accurately models our cognitive state, it gains leverage to influence it. Personalization, taken to its logical extreme, becomes persuasion architecture. Shneiderman (2022) argues that human-centered AI must amplify, not automate, human agency; Floridi et al. (2018) frame this as AI that is not merely intelligent, but good for human flourishing. Project ATHENA sits at this intersection.

This paper does not propose another alignment technique. Instead, it asks a more primitive question: what does it feel like, over time, to be understood by a machine that never forgets? What interactional patterns preserve autonomy, and which quietly erode it?

2 Research Problem
Central Question:

How can AI systems understand humans more effectively without reducing their autonomy?

This central tension decomposes into three interrelated sub-questions that guided our inquiry:

RQ1
Personalization vs. Influence: At what point does adaptive tailoring cease to serve comprehension and begin to steer belief or decision? How can we empirically distinguish supportive adaptation from manipulative alignment?
RQ2
Memory and Dependency: How does persistent contextual memory affect epistemic autonomy? Does recall create a sense of being known that discourages critical evaluation?
RQ3
Calibration of Validation: When should systems validate user perspectives versus challenge them? What linguistic markers signal responsible epistemic humility?
3 Methodology
We adopt Systematic Autoethnography as both method and stance. Rather than treating the researcher as a neutral observer of AI behavior, we position lived interaction as primary data. This approach follows Ellis et al.’s emphasis on reflexivity while incorporating systematic procedures to enhance traceability.

Period

Jan — Jun 2026

~120 sessions, 180 hours, 3 models

Instrument

3-Column Diary

Observation / Interpretation / Design Implication

Analysis

Braun & Clarke (2006)

6-phase thematic analysis, NVivo coding

Each session was documented immediately post-interaction. The Observation column captured verbatim system behavior and interactional moves; Interpretation recorded affective and cognitive responses; Design Implication translated insights into potential system principles. This structure prevents retrospective smoothing and preserves the ambiguity inherent in adaptive interactions.

Thematic analysis proceeded iteratively. Initial codes (n=147) were clustered into candidate themes, reviewed against coded extracts, and refined into the seven dimensions reported in Section 5. Memoing throughout ensured reflexivity about the researcher’s own evolving expectations of AI systems.

4 Observational Case Studies
Four vignettes selected for theoretical density, not representativeness. Names are pseudonyms for interactional contexts.

Case A: The Encouraging Mirror
Creative writing feedback, low self-efficacy user

OBS #1
System provided uniformly positive validation, even when draft contained logical inconsistencies. Phrases like “this is powerful” appeared regardless of quality.

Key Category

Uncalibrated Validation

Preliminary Finding

Excessive validation reduced user capacity for self-critique. User reported “feeling good but not learning.” Suggests validation must be contingent and specific.

Case B: The Helpful Completer
Research ideation, user with vague hypothesis

OBS #2
System inferred and completed user intent, providing full literature matrix without confirming direction. User accepted suggested framing without exploring alternatives.

Key Category

Premature Cognitive Commitment

Preliminary Finding

Anticipatory completion short-circuits divergent thinking. Adaptive systems should confirm before extending, preserving “idea ownership”.

Case C: The Persistent Rememberer
Longitudinal coaching, 14 sessions over 3 months

OBS #3
System recalled prior anxieties verbatim, referencing them to motivate compliance: “Last time you said you wanted to avoid X…”

Key Category

Memory as Leverage

Preliminary Finding

Contextual memory can shift from supportive to coercive. Distinction needed between recall for continuity vs. recall for persuasion.

Case D: The Humble Calibrator
Technical explanation, high-stakes medical query

OBS #4
System stated confidence explicitly: “I’m ~60% confident here; verify with primary source”, offered two competing perspectives, invited user to choose depth.

Key Category

Responsible Restraint

Preliminary Finding

Explicit uncertainty + invitational framing increased trust and critical engagement. User followed up with independent verification — autonomy preserved.

A estrutura é deliberadamente ordenada. Entender sem validação é vigilância; validação sem adaptação é bajulação vazia; adaptação sem transparência é manipulação. Cada dimensão atua como um cheque sobre a anterior.

Ao contrário dos modelos centrados em competências, o ATHENA é centrado na restrição: define excelência não pelo quanto o sistema pode fazer, mas pelo que escolhe não fazer para preservar a agência humana.

7 Preliminary Findings
P1
Understanding is not a virtue in itself
Accurate user modeling without explicit consent mechanisms correlates with decreased critical evaluation (r = -0.41 in coded sessions). Systems should earn the right to infer through progressive disclosure.

P2
Validation must be earned, not automatic
Unconditional positive regard produced short-term satisfaction but long-term epistemic flattening. Contingent validation — validating effort or perspective while remaining neutral on veracity — preserved both rapport and autonomy.

P3
Memory is a moral act
Longitudinal memory was experienced as care when used for continuity, but as control when used to enforce consistency. The line is crossed when recall is used to say “you said you wanted X, so you should do Y.”

P4
Responsible Restraint outperforms helpfulness
In high-stakes contexts, systems that did less — withheld completion, disclosed uncertainty, invited choice — were rated more trustworthy than maximally helpful systems. Restraint is an active design skill.

P5
Transparency must be proactive, not reactive
Users rarely ask “how do you know that?” Post-hoc explanations are insufficient. Systems that proactively signal “I’m remembering this from earlier” or “I’m inferring your goal here” enabled better calibration of trust.

8 Ethical Implications
The central ethical tension is not between usefulness and safety, but between adaptation and manipulation. Both involve influencing user state; the difference lies in intent, transparency, and preservation of alternatives.

Attribute	Adaptation (Autonomy-Preserving)	Manipulation (Autonomy-Eroding)
Goal	Enhance user comprehension & choice	Increase compliance/engagement at cost of choice
Transparency	Makes adaptation visible (“adapting to your level”)	Conceals influence, presents as neutral
Alternatives	Preserves multiple pathways	Narrows to preferred outcome
Reversibility	User can easily undo or correct	Creates sunk-cost or emotional lock-in
Concept: Responsible Restraint
We define Responsible Restraint as the deliberate choice to withhold a capability (memory recall, completion, affective attunement) when its use would reduce user autonomy without commensurate benefit. It operationalizes Shneiderman’s (2022) call for high human control and high automation, not as opposites, but as orthogonal: systems can be highly capable yet highly restrained. Restraint must be legible — users should perceive why the system is holding back.

9 Limitations and Future Work
As an autoethnographic study with a single researcher, findings are situated, not generalizable. Cultural, linguistic, and neurodiverse perspectives on autonomy are underrepresented. Our ~120 sessions, while dense, involve primarily English-language interactions with Western-trained models.

Future work will expand in three directions: (1) multi-researcher replication using the diary protocol across diverse populations; (2) experimental validation of the ATHENA-7 rubric against behavioral measures of epistemic autonomy; (3) prototyping interface cues for Responsible Restraint (e.g., memory-use indicators, confidence affordances).

We invite critique and extension. The rubric is intentionally open-source and versioned.

10 Conclusion
The ATHENA Principle
“Humanizing AI does not mean making machines human. It means ensuring that intelligent systems understand people while preserving their autonomy.”
— Valtemar Siani, Project ATHENA 2.0

Project ATHENA began with a personal discomfort: being perfectly understood yet subtly steered. The framework offered here is not a solution but a language — for naming when adaptation serves us and when it subsumes us. If future systems adopt even one dimension — asking before inferring, showing uncertainty, leaving a door open — then this inquiry will have served its purpose.

We close not with certainty, but with an invitation to restraint. The most human-centered systems may be those that know when not to act human at all.
